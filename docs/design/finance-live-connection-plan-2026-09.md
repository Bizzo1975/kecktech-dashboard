# Finance and Resource Hub — live connection plan

**Research date:** 2026-09-14. **Status:** design and source inventory; no account connection is active or implied. **Rule:** routine operation requires no manual file import. Initial account consent, provider enrollment, occasional renewed consent, and review of exceptions may require a person. A source without a working automated route remains visibly **unconnected**; neither a spreadsheet value nor an inferred recurring transaction is presented as a live bill.

## What the workbook actually identifies

The current OneDrive workbook has 74 proposed bill/expense automation rows across housing (10), transportation (9), non-mortgage debt (9), utilities (8), healthcare (7), subscriptions (7), discretionary/other (12), family/care (4), insurance (3), financial goals (3), and food (2). All 74 `Provider / Sender` cells are blank. Setup statuses are `Provider needed` (52), `Rules not configured` (13), and `Review needed` (9). Its recommended routes count email/Power Automate (35), bank/card CSV and rules or review (22), portal CSV/API with email fallback (11), email or portal export (3), and bank CSV/transfer rule (3). **These are suggestions, not verified connections.** Bank/card CSV in those suggestions conflicts with the operator's no-manual-import requirement; replace it with a live account feed or an automated bank-provided delivery. The workbook has no transaction rows. Private provider/account identifiers belong in an encrypted runtime registry outside Git, with only source IDs in the app database.

## Connection architecture

1. **Financial truth:** permissioned bank/card/loan aggregation or an institution's supported API for posted transactions and balances. Use a server-side token vault. The bank feed tells us whether money moved; it rarely supplies the complete utility bill, grocery basket, insurance declaration, or tax evidence. Plaid Transactions covers checking/savings, credit, and some loan transaction histories; `/transactions/sync` returns added, modified, and removed records, triggered by `SYNC_UPDATES_AVAILABLE`. Institution/product coverage, freshness, production access, and pricing must be verified per account, not assumed.[P1][P2][P3]
2. **Document truth:** provider's supported API, customer-authorized data share, or automatically delivered email body/attachment for amount due, service period, line items, rates, taxes, policy/contract terms, and source PDF. Microsoft Graph supports mail-folder delta and attachment retrieval; Gmail supports message/history/attachment retrieval. Use an allowlisted sender plus provider/account identifiers and document signatures, never sender address alone. Quarantine unknown attachments and parse failures. An emailed notice containing only a portal link is not the bill document.[M1][M2][G1]
3. **Operational truth:** existing Marketlist, ERPNext, utility meter, and observability APIs, scoped read-only at first. These provide item quantities, business invoices, energy use, or equipment load; they are neither substitutes for bank posting nor duplicate cash expense events.[F1][E1]
4. **Orchestration:** n8n or a dedicated worker receives webhooks and runs schedules, while hub adapters own parsing, versioned mapping, matching, idempotency, audit, and source freshness. No financial tokens or full bill contents in general workflow logs. Provider writes, payments, plan changes, and negotiations remain separate, reviewable actions.

**Aggregator selection is per institution, not one universal bet.** Evaluate Plaid first for Transactions/Liabilities and incremental webhooks, then test MX, Akoya/FDX, or SimpleFIN where an institution or commercial agreement makes them a better fit. MX exposes institution capability fields and transaction endpoints; Akoya exposes permissioned FDX transactions; SimpleFIN defines a read-only account/transaction protocol. None proves coverage of these still-unnamed accounts, and a second aggregator introduces duplicate-event and token/consent complexity. Record product-level coverage, current terms, costs, refresh latency, historical depth, pending behavior, and reauthentication before selecting.[X1][X2][X3] The CFPB's personal-financial-data-rights compliance dates were stayed as of the official page checked; do not assume the rule currently forces any specific institution to provide an API.[C1]

Preferred acquisition order for each source: supported direct API/OAuth → permissioned aggregator/standard customer-data share → automatically delivered structured email or attached PDF/CSV → supported provider-to-mailbox scheduled report → approved, read-only browser automation only if the provider's terms and MFA flow allow it. Site automation is fragile and must never store a password in code, bypass MFA/CAPTCHA, simulate success, or silently continue after a page change. If all options fail, mark the feed unsupported and show its missing coverage. Do not solve this with a manual import requirement.

## Required connection families and acquisition plans

### Money and obligations

- **Household checking, savings, debit, and credit cards:** live Transactions feed for posted/pending lifecycle, balances where supported, account IDs, and transfer matching; Statements product only where eligible for closing documents. Plaid Statements presently covers *depository* statements only, not credit-card PDFs, and is not universally available. For card statement PDFs, use card-issuer automated email/API or supported portal delivery. Compare every closed statement balance with the transaction register; expose gaps and stale sync. One credential grant per institution may expose multiple selected accounts, but account-level consent and coverage must be checked.[P1][P4]
- **Mortgage, auto, student, personal loans, BNPL, and card liabilities:** use supported liability/servicer API for principal, rate, minimum, due date, escrow, and payoff terms; bank-feed payment alone proves only cash outflow. Plaid Liabilities supports credit cards, mortgages, student loans, and PayPal credit in supported institutions; it does **not** provide detailed card transactions and should not be assumed to cover auto or every private loan. Use servicer email statements or direct API where needed.[P5]
- **Payroll, benefits, reimbursements, dividends, interest, farm/business receipts, and other income:** bank feed confirms net deposits. Gross pay, deductions, tax withholding, employer benefits, and reimbursement purpose require payroll/HR API or automatically sent paystubs/earnings statements. Business revenue and invoice status come from ERPNext; processor payouts must match both ERPNext invoices and bank credits. Do not estimate gross income from net deposits.
- **Scheduled savings and transfers:** bank feed plus matched counterparty account confirms movement; financial goal progress is not a household expense. Investment/retirement balances and holdings need a separate supported investment connector or custodian API, if in scope.[P6]
- **Taxes and official documents:** payroll/ERPNext/merchant/servicer documents arrive by provider email/API where offered; record W-2, 1099, property tax, mortgage interest, insurance, charitable, and business source-document metadata in private storage. No claim that IRS transcripts or every tax form can be auto-pulled. Year-end package is incomplete until missing forms and reconciliations are cleared.

### The workbook's 74 expense lines

- **Housing (10):** mortgage/rent, property tax, HOA, maintenance, and related obligations need servicer/landlord/municipal/HOA bill API or emailed statements. Bank posting confirms payment, not the service period or escrow components. A direct institution/portal route is **[ NEEDS PROVIDER CHECK ]** for each line.
- **Utilities (8):** electric, gas, water, sewer, trash, communications and similar bills use utility customer API or Green Button *Connect My Data* for interval usage where supported, plus emailed/portal bill for dollars and tariff. Green Button *Download My Data* alone is user download and does not meet unattended acquisition. Match meter period to invoice and bank debit.[U1]
- **Transportation (9):** auto loan/lease, insurance, fuel, maintenance, registration, tolls and parking need loan/insurer/municipal/toll emails or APIs; retail fuel and repairs come from bank/card plus emailed merchant receipts. Odometer/mileage for business use needs a separate authorized tracker; it cannot be deduced from purchases.
- **Non-mortgage debt (9):** use liability details, billing emails, and account transactions; separate interest, fees, principal, and balance transfers. An automatic payment on a checking account is not a second expense when the card purchase was already counted.
- **Healthcare (7):** insurance premiums and provider bills require insurer/provider email or API, while bank/card feeds confirm payment. Medical claims/EOBs and HSA/FSA activity are separate, highly sensitive feeds requiring explicit scope and retention. An EOB is not a paid bill.
- **Subscriptions (7):** bank recurring-stream detection discovers candidates; provider invoices/emails establish plan, quantity, renewal, and cancellation terms. A detected charge is not authorization to cancel or negotiate.
- **Discretionary/other (12), family/care (4), food (2):** transaction feeds cover cash movement; merchant email receipts/Marketlist purchase records provide item detail and quality/price provenance. Cash purchases require a compatible automatic source such as a digital receipt; otherwise coverage is honestly incomplete. Do not silently manufacture a receipt.
- **Insurance (3):** declarations, premium changes, coverage, deductibles, and renewals need insurer documents/API/email; bank feeds only verify payment. Savings advice must compare coverage, not just premium.
- **Financial goals (3):** match transfers/investment contributions to destination accounts; do not count internal transfers as spend or income.

### Existing apps, homestead, and datacenter

- **Marketlist:** read-only, household-authorized API for pantry, shopping trips, receipt lines, price history, garden harvest, and stock changes. Match each trip/receipt total to a bank/card transaction; cash expense is counted once. Confirm route schema/auth against current deployment before connecting. Its price history is observed store/product data, not a universal real-time grocery price API.
- **ERPNext:** read-only Frappe REST access to posted sales/purchase invoices, payment entries, expense claims, chart of accounts, assets, inventory, cost centers, and company IDs as deployed. Use a least-privilege service identity; incremental pull by stable document ID/modification timestamp with a reconciliation sweep. ERPNext owns business books; hub owns cross-scope analysis.[F1]
- **n8n and Kecktech Ops:** workflow status/failed jobs and observability read APIs feed sync health and costs, not financial entries. Cost allocation needs tagged assets, service ownership, invoices, and measured usage. Planned datacenter remains a scenario until actual services and meters exist.
- **Homestead inventory, production, and procurement:** Marketlist garden/inventory plus future livestock, seed, feed, harvest, spoilage, sales, and purchasing source systems. Each lot has quantity/unit, location, owner scope, purchase/harvest provenance, and quality evidence. Bank or ERPNext validates cash impact; biological yield alone is not income.
- **Solar, geothermal, grid, water, fuel, and datacenter equipment:** utility Connect My Data or customer API for usage, inverter/controller and building-automation APIs for generation/thermal flow, and existing telemetry for IT/facility energy where actually installed. Join measurements to tariff-versioned bills and service periods; quantify savings only after baseline normalization. Prometheus has a query API, but a metric's presence, calibration, and ownership must be checked on site.[U1][O1]
- **Local food suppliers and product quality:** Marketlist's dated receipts and supplier invoices are first choice. Public catalogs or product databases can enrich product/quality metadata, but are not proof of local availability, freshness, or the paid price. A supplier quote needs expiry, delivery cost, unit, quality claim, and source.

## Private provider registry required before implementation

For each of the 74 workbook lines **and each financial account or operational source**, capture outside Git: opaque source ID; legal owner and visibility scope; provider name and account alias (no full account number in design docs); website; bill/statement sender domain; billing cadence and expected date; document format and sample; available API/OAuth/aggregator products; delivery settings; consent owner/date/expiry; permitted automation method and terms; MFA behavior; required fields; historical range; freshness target; failure contact; and whether the provider serves multiple lines. A single provider may cover many lines, while one line may need account, bill, and receipt sources. Deduplicate connections by actual provider account, not spreadsheet line count.

For every candidate, record a proof result: `verified live`, `supported but not connected`, `email delivery configured`, `site automation approved`, `unsupported`, or `unknown`; link official documentation or observed operator settings. Do not store credentials in the registry. The current workbook has **zero provider names**, so no institution-specific coverage or biller API can honestly be certified yet.

## Build sequence and acceptance

1. **Inventory and coverage proof:** privately identify financial institutions, mailbox provider(s), actual billers, payroll provider, ERPNext company, Marketlist household, and installed meters. Check each institution/product in the aggregator's current coverage tool; inspect each biller's customer data and email-delivery settings. Produce a provider-by-provider route decision and gap list. No manual-import fallback is accepted.
2. **Connection foundation:** encrypted token vault, per-source consent/ownership, webhook signature verification, mailbox least-privilege scopes, deduplication keys, immutable raw evidence hashes, parser version, retry/dead-letter queue, source freshness, revoke/disconnect, and deletion/retention controls. Keep financial data out of Git, Notion, logs, and model prompts.
3. **First vertical slice:** one checking account and one credit-card account live feed, one emailed bill attachment, and one Marketlist receipt. Demonstrate posted updates/reversals, bill-to-payment and card-payment transfer matching, source drilldown, and a closed-period balance check with no manual file import. A missed webhook must recover by polling; a malformed attachment must raise an exception, not fabricate a number.
4. **Expand by provider family:** utility and insurance documents; payroll gross-to-net; debt terms; ERPNext; homestead inventory; energy meters. Close each family only after observed live data, expected cadence, statement tie-out, permission review, and outage/staleness tests.

**Decision boundary:** an unattended system can still require one-time consent and human review of ambiguous matches. If a provider exposes neither a supported API/data share nor automated document delivery and approved site access, this plan cannot truthfully promise complete live coverage. The dashboard must show that gap and exclude it from “all expenses connected” claims until the provider path changes.
