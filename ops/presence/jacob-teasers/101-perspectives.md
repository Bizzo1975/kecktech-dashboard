---
title: "101 Perspectives of Me"
summary: "I asked 101 people the same 35 questions about me — after locking my own answers in a sealed file. The gap between the two is the book."
slug: 101-perspectives
---

Here is the experiment. Thirty-five questions about one man. One hundred and one people answering them — strangers who met me once, colleagues, old friends, family. And me answering the same questions twice: once honestly, and once as a prediction of what the crowd would say.

The order matters more than anything else in this project. My answers were written and locked before a single survey went out, timestamped and hashed, sealed against the very human temptation to quietly revise myself once the data started arriving. The book's credibility rests entirely on that lock. Anyone can be perceptive in hindsight.

The chapters move outside in. First the stranger's thirty-second read of a man across a room, then behavior as witnessed against behavior as intended, then the leap people make from what you do to what they think you *are*, then the mirror, then the shadow — the contradictions, the masks, the conversations nobody has with me. It ends with the question the respondents wished they could ask.

What I expected was a portrait. What I got was a shape. Some questions came back with high agreement and no surprise — fine, we agree on this, and it's a relief. Others came back with the room in perfect consensus about something I had never once seen in myself. Those are the chapters that hurt, and they are the best ones. And a few came back as pure chaos, one hundred and one people looking at the same man and reporting a hundred different weather systems.

There is a smaller finding underneath all of it, and it's the reason I kept going when the results got uncomfortable. The people closest to me had been quietly running a protocol around my behavior for years — timing their honesty to my recovery curve, reading a signal I never knew I was broadcasting. Every one of those adjustments was a real interaction shaped by a model of me I never confirmed or denied.

You don't interact with people. You interact with their model of you. And the model gets updated whether or not you're in the room.
