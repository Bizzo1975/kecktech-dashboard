title: "Yours, Mine, and the Truth"
summary: "A comedy about a three-year marriage told three ways — his version, her version, and the deflating truth — narrated by the man who had to learn both languages."
slug: yours-mine-and-the-truth
---

Every story from a bad marriage exists in two versions, and both of them are wrong.

Sam knows this better than anyone, because he married into the wreckage. His wife has an ex-husband. The ex-husband has a version of everything. His wife has her own version of everything, and hers is forensic, damning, and quietly missing all the parts where she contributed. Sam has heard both, cross-examined both, and reconstructed what actually happened — which is always smaller, dumber, and more human than either story. He has become the family interpreter, translating one ex to the other so they can raise their daughter without setting anything on fire.

Then all of them end up as volunteer chaperones at a church summer camp for a week, along with the ex-husband's new girlfriend, and Sam appoints himself her orientation officer. The stories he tells her over those seven days are the book.

So you get his version, epic and blameless, in which a raccoon becomes at least three raccoons, possibly organized. You get her version, surgical and devastating. And then you get the truth, which usually takes one paragraph and turns out to be a mislabeled calendar invite. The rule I set for myself was that the truth is never allowed to make either of them a villain. It's only allowed to make them understandable — the kind of understandable where you finish the paragraph and think, *oh no, I've done that.*

Meanwhile the camp keeps happening. New misunderstandings detonate in real time, in front of children and clergy, while everyone performs patience and grace over six-year-old grudges. And somewhere around midweek, the interpreter — fluent, tenured, doing everyone a kindness — mistranslates his own marriage.

That's the part I wrote the book for. Fluency in someone else's failures does not make you fluent in your own. There is no cure for this. There is only translation.
